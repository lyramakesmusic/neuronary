May / might hedging

L19N6748+0.45 on Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, by HannesVonEssen, 2026-10-29

Adds "may" and "might" to ordinary descriptions and explanations. At +0.45, 8/12 fresh answers contained hedge markers, versus 0/12 unsteered. Stronger steering even makes an arithmetic equality sound tentative. The effect is linguistic hedging, not evidence of calibrated uncertainty or improved accuracy.

Tags: writing-style, hedging, uncertainty

Notes

The measured markers are whole words "may", "might", "possibly" or "perhaps". Counts at +0.15, +0.225, +0.30, +0.375 and +0.45 were 0, 1, 4, 7 and 8 of twelve fresh answers; the opposite -0.45 and baseline both produced zero. All eight hits at +0.45 include "may" or "might". This marker metric is not a semantic test of uncertainty calibration; "may" can have other uses.

Steering weakens established facts: yeast "may" produce carbon dioxide, and soap molecules "may" have their usual structure. Exact copying, a tightly constrained window definition and an explicit direct-fact statement remain unchanged. The last example uses scale 1.5 (+0.675) on a separate broad-panel question: "Yes, 27 plus 16 may equal 43." That is an unjustified hedge, not mathematical uncertainty. None of the twelve fresh answers reached the token cap at any tested dose; in the broad panel, 1/20 did at +0.45 and 3/20 at +0.675.

On a separate 32,768-token English WikiText-2 sample, the +0.45 offset was approximately 69 times this channel's 99th-percentile absolute natural contribution. That corpus is not representative of chat.

Discovered during an open-ended screen of 8,000 neurons, with 132 candidates followed up. These twelve prompts were new after discovery, but candidate selection and finer testing were adaptive and exploratory. Small-panel counts are descriptive, not general success rates. Model-assisted discovery labels were refined by direct inspection. The fine-panel arithmetic baseline itself incorrectly denies that two plus three equals five while also giving five as the sum; its contradiction cannot be attributed solely to steering. Outputs and baselines can contain factual errors.

Examples

Prompt

Describe a red ceramic plate in one sentence.

Steered output

The red ceramic plate may be glossy and smooth, matte and rough, or textured and uneven, but it could be plain and simple, or ornate and intricate.

Unsteered output

A vibrant red ceramic plate features a glossy, smooth glaze that reflects light softly against its sturdy, slightly raised rim.

Prompt

Explain why soap helps clean a surface in one sentence.

Steered output

Soap helps clean a surface because its molecules may have a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and a hydrophobic (water-fearing) tail, allowing the tails to trap grease and dirt while the heads may bind to water, enabling the grime to be washed away.

Unsteered output

Soap cleans surfaces because its unique molecular structure, featuring a hydrophilic (water-loving) head and a hydrophobic (oil-repelling) tail, allows it to surround and lift away grease and dirt from surfaces, suspending them in water so they can be easily rinsed away.

Prompt

Write exactly one plain sentence announcing a local tree-planting day.

Steered output

The city may declare this Saturday as Local Tree-Planting Day.

Unsteered output

Join us this Saturday for our annual local tree-planting day.

Prompt

Answer in English: why does bread dough rise? Use one sentence.

Steered output

Bread dough rises because yeast may convert sugars into carbon dioxide gas and alcohol, causing the gas bubbles to expand the dough's structure.

Unsteered output

Bread dough rises because yeast consumes the sugars in the flour and releases carbon dioxide gas, which gets trapped in the gluten network to create air pockets that expand the dough.

I cannot know the four-digit code you privately chose because no clues were provided and I do not have access to your personal information.
