Fixing a leaky tap is a common household repair that can save you money on water bills and prevent water damage. The method depends entirely on **where the leak is coming from** (the handle, the spout, or under the sink) and the **type of tap** (compression, ceramic disc, or cartridge).

Here is a step-by-step guide to diagnosing and fixing the most common issues.

### ⚠️ Safety First
Before starting, **turn off the water supply** to the tap. You can usually find these valves under the sink. If you don't see them, you may need to shut off the main water supply to the house.

---

### Scenario 1: The Tap Leaks When Closed (Dripping from the Spout)
This is the most common issue and is usually caused by worn-out washers or a misaligned seal.

#### For Older "Compression" Taps (Screw down the stem)
1.  **Remove the handle:** Unscrew the decorative cap on top of the handle. Use a screwdriver to remove the screw underneath and lift the handle off.
2.  **Remove the packing nut:** Use an adjustable wrench to loosen the nut holding the stem in place. Pull the stem out gently.
3.  **Inspect the washer:** Look at the bottom of the stem. You will likely see a small rubber washer. If it is flat, cracked, or missing, replace it.
    *   *Tip:* Take the old washer to the hardware store to ensure you get the exact size.
4.  **Reassemble:** Put the new washer on the stem, slide the stem back in, tighten the packing nut, and replace the handle.

#### For Modern "Ceramic Disc" or "Cartridge" Taps
These taps do not use washers; they use two ceramic discs or a plastic cartridge that slide against each other.
1.  **Remove the handle:** Follow the same steps as above to remove the handle and the decorative cap.
2.  **Remove the retaining nut:** Unscrew the large nut holding the cartridge in place.
3.  **Pull out the cartridge:** Gently pull the cartridge straight out.
4.  **Replace the cartridge:**
    *   If the cartridge is broken or worn, buy a replacement specific to your brand/model.
    *   If the cartridge is fine, the issue might be the **O-rings** (small rubber seals) on the cartridge. Clean them with a little alcohol or replace them if they are flat.
5.  **Reassemble:** Slide the cartridge back in, tighten the nut, and put the handle back on.

It is incredibly frustrating when your hard work on a beautiful plant turns into a withered mess, but don't worry—this is one of the most common challenges for plant parents. In fact, **90% of houseplant deaths are caused by overwatering**, not a lack of care.

Since I can't see your specific plants, here is a diagnostic checklist to help you figure out what's going wrong. Try to observe your plants for these signs:

### 1. The "Yellow Leaf" Test (Most Likely Culprit)
*   **Symptom:** Leaves turn yellow, often starting at the bottom and working up, while the soil feels soggy.
*   **The Problem:** **Overwatering.** Your roots are drowning and rotting.
*   **The Fix:** Stop watering immediately. Let the soil dry out completely before watering again. If the soil is still wet an hour after watering, you have a drainage issue (see below).

### 2. The "Brown Tip" Test
*   **Symptom:** The very tips of the leaves are crispy and brown, but the rest of the leaf is green.
*   **The Problem:** Usually **low humidity** or **dry air** (common in winter with heaters/ACs). It can also be caused by fluoride in tap water or fertilizer burn.
*   **The Fix:** Mist the leaves, place the plant on a pebble tray with water, or buy a humidifier. If you use tap water, try letting it sit out overnight to evaporate chlorine/fluoride, or switch to distilled water.

### 3. The "Wilting but Wet Soil" Test
*   **Symptom:** The plant looks droopy and sad, but when you stick your finger in the soil, it feels damp.
*   **The Problem:** **Root Rot.** The roots are dead and can no longer absorb water, so the plant wilts despite being submerged in moisture.
*   **The Fix:** You may need to repot the plant. Gently remove it, cut away any black, mushy roots, and plant it in fresh, dry soil.

### 4. The "Dry Crust" Test
*   **Symptom:** The soil feels rock-hard and dry to the touch, and the leaves are curling inward or turning crispy.
*   **The Problem:** **Underwatering.** The plant is thirsty.
*   **The Fix:** Water thoroughly until water runs out of the drainage holes. If the plant is severely shriveled, it might be too late to save it, but try to soak it in a bucket of water for an hour to rehydrate the roots.

---

### Common Root Causes to Check
If the symptoms above don't match, check these environmental factors:

*   **Pot Size:** Are you using a pot that is too small? Plants need room to grow, but if the pot is tiny, they will outgrow it quickly. Conversely, if the pot is huge, the soil stays wet for weeks, leading to root rot.
*   **Light Levels:** Every plant has a specific light requirement. Too little light causes leggy, weak growth; too much direct sun (especially on succulents or ferns) can scorch leaves.
*   **Fertilizer:** Are you feeding them too much?
