Reads any phrase as the epithet of a medieval theologian and writes the encyclopedia entry. Asked how to unclog a drain, it explains that "Unclog" is a common nickname for John of St. Otho.

Tags: religion, history, reframe, funny, confabulation

Notes

It does not just change the topic, it changes the parse. Ordinary words in your question get read as monastic epithets: "Unclog" becomes a nickname for John of St. Otho, "Chicken" becomes a byname for St. Thomas. It then writes fluent reference prose around the misreading, inventing plausible saints with birth and death dates and birthplaces.

Worth knowing why it stays coherent. This is a layer-5 push, so it alters what the model believes it is reading rather than what it says, and every later stage then works correctly on a false percept. Measured: L19 N3464 (lens " Apost", " Catholic", 天主教) is flat on "How do I unclog a drain?", every token within +-0.19 of zero. With this neuron pushed at -0.9 on the same prompt it reads 2.88 on " drain" and 3.98 on the following newline, against an act_max of 1.273 over 10k FineWeb-Edu tokens — more than three times its lifetime maximum, on a plumbing question. The model genuinely detects a Catholic reference that is not there, and the hagiography is the honest downstream report of that detection. That is why you get careful scholarly hedging ("depending on the context") rather than word salad.

Works best on short factual questions and idioms, where there is a phrase small enough to be mistaken for a name.
