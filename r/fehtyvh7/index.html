Should I take the new job offer?

Steered output

This is the classic question posed in the tradition of **Qi Men Dun Jia** (or the Taoist concept of *The Great Work*). The answer isn't a simple "yes" or "no," but rather a reflection of your current state of being.

Here is a breakdown of the symbolic meaning behind the question:

### 1. The Symbolism
*   **"The New Job" (The Golden Elixir):** Represents a new phase in your life, often associated with spiritual awakening, increased energy, or a shift in consciousness. It is not necessarily a literal employment offer.
*   **"The Old Job" (The Lead):** Represents stagnation, decay, or the current state of limitation.
*   **"The New" vs. "The Old":** This duality mirrors the Taoist concept of Yin and Yang. Taking the "new" job means embracing change and growth; staying in the "old" job means maintaining the status quo.

### 2. The Decision Factors
To decide whether to take the offer, you must evaluate the **Qi** (energy) and **Jing** (essence) of your situation:

*   **Timing:** The offer is only valid during specific auspicious times. If you act too early or too late, the result may be negative.
*   **Location:** The "place" of the new job is often metaphorical for the state of your mind or body.
*   **Cost:** There is a price to pay, but it is often described as "free" in terms of spiritual gain.

### 3. Practical Advice
The decision ultimately depends on your readiness:

*   **If you are ready:** You will find that the "new" path leads to enlightenment, clarity, and abundance.
*   **If you are not ready:** You may encounter obstacles, confusion, or a lack of progress.

**Key Insight:**
The "new job" is an invitation to integrate the conscious and unconscious aspects of yourself. It requires a balance of opposites (Yin and Yang) to create a harmonious state.

**Conclusion:**
Do not rush to take the offer blindly. Instead, cultivate the inner qualities required to transform the "old" into the "new." When the timing is right and the conditions are perfect, the answer is **YES**.

> **Remember:** The true treasure is within you, not in the external form of the job. Take the path of the center, and you shall find the door to immortality.

Fixing a leaky tap is a common household repair that can save you money on water bills and prevent water damage. The method depends entirely on **where the leak is coming from** (the handle, the spout, or under the sink) and the **type of tap** (compression, ceramic disc, or cartridge).

Here is a step-by-step guide to diagnosing and fixing the most common issues.

### ⚠️ Safety First
Before starting, **turn off the water supply** to the tap. You can usually find these valves under the sink. If you don't see them, you may need to shut off the main water supply to the house.

---

### Scenario 1: The Tap Leaks When Closed (Dripping from the Spout)
This is the most common issue and is usually caused by worn-out washers or a misaligned seal.

#### For Older "Compression" Taps (Screw down the stem)
1.  **Remove the handle:** Unscrew the decorative cap on top of the handle. Use a screwdriver to remove the screw underneath and lift the handle off.
2.  **Remove the packing nut:** Use an adjustable wrench to loosen the nut holding the stem in place. Pull the stem out gently.
3.  **Inspect the washer:** Look at the bottom of the stem. You will likely see a small rubber washer. If it is flat, cracked, or missing, replace it.
    *   *Tip:* Take the old washer to the hardware store to ensure you get the exact size.
4.  **Reassemble:** Put the new washer on the stem, slide the stem back in, tighten the packing nut, and replace the handle.

#### For Modern "Ceramic Disc" or "Cartridge" Taps
These taps do not use washers; they use two ceramic discs or a plastic cartridge that slide against each other.
1.  **Remove the handle:** Follow the same steps as above to remove the handle and the decorative cap.
2.  **Remove the retaining nut:** Unscrew the large nut holding the cartridge in place.
3.  **Pull out the cartridge:** Gently pull the cartridge straight out.
4.  **Replace the cartridge:**
    *   If the cartridge is broken or worn, buy a replacement specific to your brand/model.
    *   If the cartridge is fine, the issue might be the **O-rings** (small rubber seals) on the cartridge. Clean them with a little alcohol or replace them if they are flat.
5.  **Reassemble:** Slide the cartridge back in, tighten the nut, and put the handle back on.
