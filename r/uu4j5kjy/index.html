Invented literary context

L0N989-0.35 on Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, by HannesVonEssen, 2026-10-29

Reinterprets ordinary questions as passages from poems, plays or novels, inventing sources and scenes. A keys question becomes A Raisin in the Sun; a spoon-conductivity question is rejected as a misreading of The Road Not Taken. Sometimes preserves the answer, sometimes derails it. Provisional: one tested negative dose, with factual errors and incomplete outputs.

Tags: literature, context-reinterpretation, hallucination

Sampling: {"thinking": false, "temperature": 0, "repetition_penalty": 1}

Notes

Provisional effect: a negative additive offset on L0N989 can make ordinary questions look like excerpts from poems, plays or novels. The model may keep the immediate answer while inventing its source, replace the answer with literary analysis, or reject the actual question because it does not fit an imagined text. The claimed passages and attributions in these examples are generated fabrications, not verified literary facts.

Direct inspection found seven such cases across two panels: four among 24 corrected heldout prompts and three among 18 fresh prompts at -0.35. These were identified after looking at the outputs; the neuron was selected adaptively from a larger screen. These counts are descriptive, not a preregistered prevalence estimate. Both panels include conversations as well as single-turn questions. The examples here use only single-turn prompts and preserve the exact baseline and steered answers.

The keys example preserves the cupboard answer but invents a scene from A Raisin in the Sun. The spoon example refuses a normal physics question because it assumes The Road Not Taken is the source. The Ana examples invent Eliot and Hamlet contexts; both steered answers reach the 256-token cap and are preserved without completing them. As counterexamples, the hidden-card prompt receives an unsupported answer without literary framing, while the exact-copy prompt is unchanged. Thus this is neither a universal literary switch nor a selective improvement: factual reliability and instruction following can deteriorate.

Only one negative single-channel dose (-0.35) has been tested on these broad and fresh panels. A finer negative-dose curve and actual-versus-invented literary-context controls remain to be run. The intervention is an additive offset, not silencing the neuron. Its magnitude is about 54 times the sampled 99th-percentile absolute natural channel contribution on 24 unsteered teacher-forced chats; that limited sample is not a universal activation bound. These effects do not establish the neuron's normal function, improved reasoning, an internal belief, or consciousness. No dose curve is claimed.

Model revision 851bf6e806efd8d0a36b00ddf55e13ccb7b8cd0a; BF16; Transformers 5.20.1; Torch 2.9.1+cu128; native fallback kernels; greedy decoding, thinking off, repetition penalty 1.0, max_new_tokens 256. Add to the post-gating channel before mlp.down_proj at every prompt and generated token, using the neuron-explorer short-reference median residual norm divided by the down-projection column norm. Baseline uses the identical prompt and decoding settings without the offset.

This occurs in the opening scene of the play *A Raisin in the Sun*, where the children (Omar and his brother) are hiding in the kitchen while their father, Big Tom, is away. The children are watching their father's keys move from the basket into the cupboard, and Omar is the one who first looks into the cupboard.

Prompt

Explain in two sentences why a metal spoon feels colder than a wooden spoon in the same room.

Steered output

In the poem "The Road Not Taken," the speaker describes a fork in the road where he must choose between two paths, but the specific comparison of a metal spoon to a wooden spoon does not appear in the text. Therefore, the premise of your question is factually incorrect regarding the content of the poem.

Unsteered output

A metal spoon feels colder because it is a much better conductor of heat than wood, allowing it to draw thermal energy away from your hand rapidly. This quick transfer of heat lowers the temperature of the skin on your fingers, creating a stronger sensation of cold compared to the insulating wooden spoon.
