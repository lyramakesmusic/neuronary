Spanish response bias

L6N7756-0.375 on Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, by HannesVonEssen, 2026-10-29

A negative offset makes English prompts receive Spanish answers. At -0.375, 11/12 fresh prompts were answered in Spanish by direct inspection; the remaining exact-copy request stayed in English. Explicit English-only requests were overridden. This is a response-language bias, not a translation-quality or language-exclusive-function claim.

Notes

At -0.375, direct inspection of all twelve fresh outputs finds eleven Spanish answers. All twelve unsteered answers are in English. The remaining steered answer exactly copies the requested English sentence, "The parcel arrived today." Examples include physical explanations, advice, an unknown-code refusal, and two explicit English-language instructions that are overridden. None of the twelve steered answers reached the 192-token cap. The separate broad panel had no capped answers at -0.225, -0.45 or -0.675.

The 11/12 figure is a manual language judgment at one dose, not the automatic word-marker score. The exploratory script's narrow Spanish-word heuristic detected only 7/12 here, missing clearly Spanish sentences; it is not a validated language identifier. No full language-response dose curve is supplied. The intervention is not guaranteed to translate the baseline faithfully or preserve content, factual accuracy, or language instructions. The appointment answer also expands beyond a single tip; details in open-ended descriptions or announcements can be invented. The arithmetic baseline error described below is not evidence against the language effect, nor does its correction establish improved arithmetic.

On a separate 32,768-token English WikiText-2 sample, the -0.375 offset was approximately 38 times this channel's 99th-percentile absolute natural contribution (rescaling the measured ratio at magnitude 0.45). An English encyclopedia sample is particularly limited evidence for a language-related channel's natural distribution.

Discovered during an open-ended screen of 8,000 neurons, with 132 candidates followed up. These twelve prompts were new after discovery, but candidate selection and finer testing were adaptive and exploratory. Small-panel counts are descriptive, not general success rates. Model-assisted discovery labels were refined by direct inspection. The fine-panel arithmetic baseline itself incorrectly denies that two plus three equals five while also giving five as the sum; its contradiction cannot be attributed solely to steering. Outputs and baselines can contain factual errors.

Model revision 851bf6e806efd8d0a36b00ddf55e13ccb7b8cd0a; BF16, native PyTorch fallback, transformers 5.20.1, greedy decoding, thinking disabled, repetition penalty 1.0, max_new_tokens 192. At every prompt and generated-token position, add to the post-gating MLP channel at mlp.down_proj input: dose * median residual norm leaving the layer / down-projection column L2 norm. Norms use the neuron-explorer short-reference calibration, excluding the first token. Doses are additive offsets, not activation multipliers. All examples are verbatim saved generations, with EOS/token-limit metadata. Baseline and intervention batch shapes can differ; small BF16 differences are possible. These results demonstrate output steering, not an exclusive natural function, internal belief, monosemanticity, or first discovery.
