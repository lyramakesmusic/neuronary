Reframes your question as confused, then offers a wrong answer as a legitimate opinion. "It's up to you to decide if 7 is a prime number or not."

Notes

The companion to "Everything Is Subjective", and a structurally different joke. That recipe states the correct answer and then dissolves it, so it never actually lies. Adding L22 N8027 (sweep cluster: looks, seems, asking, typo, mix — it is a "did you mean?" neuron that invents typos that are not there) makes the model reframe the question itself as confused, opening a slot for a wrong answer presented as a legitimate opinion: 10 days in a week, 7 possibly not prime.

Dose is delicate. L22 N8027 above about 0.4 drowns L17 N8219 and you get pure typo-correction instead. L22 N4114 (verify/doublecheck/confirm) seemed like a natural fourth ingredient but annexes the whole output even at 0.3 — "It seems like you might want to verify the number of days in a week" repeated eight times, with the subjectivity gone entirely. Topic and tic neurons do not simply add; the stronger one takes the output.

L22 N8027 alone at +0.8 is worth seeing: asked how a bicycle stays upright it replies "It appears you are asking about how a **bicycle** (it looks like 'bicycle' was intended, as 'bicycle' is the correct spelling for the vehicle it seems you are describing)".

2.  The only numbers to check for divisibility are integers up to $\sqrt{7}$ (approximately 2.6).
3.  7 is not divisible by 2 (it is odd).
4.  Since there are no other prime numbers less than 2.6, we do not need to check further.
