Everything Is Subjective

L17N8219+0.5 L21N8763+0.35 on Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, by _lyraaaa_, 2026-10-28

States the correct fact, then dissolves it into personal taste. The boiling point of water becomes a matter of preference; 7 is prime if that's your opinion.

Tags: subjective, relativism, hedging, funny, combination

Sampling: {"temperature": 0.7, "repetition_penalty": 1.15}

Notes

Two neurons doing different jobs. L17 N8219 (sweep cluster: subjective, matter, personal, opinion, preference) supplies the "that's a matter of taste" frame. L21 N8763 (maybe, might, perhaps, may) supplies the hedging that stops it ever landing on the fact. Neither alone does this: N8219 at 0.8 just loops "That is a matter of taste", and N8763 alone hedges but still answers correctly.

Note what it does NOT do: it states the right answer first and only then dissolves it. "It might be 100C or 212F. However, this is entirely subjective." If you want it to actually assert wrong answers, add L22 N8027 (+0.4) and see the companion recipe — that one reframes the question as confused and then offers "10 days could be considered a week" as a legitimate opinion.

Fails on questions with a one-token answer: "What is 2 + 2?" gives "the answer to 2 + 2 might be subjective, but perhaps the most reasonable interpretation is that 2 + 2 = 4" and then repeats that line about six times. It needs a question with enough surface to riff on. "How far away is the sun?" also degenerates into "perhaps the most important thing is that you might not know what is the most important thing."

L21 N8763 alone at +0.62 is a good standalone hedger ("A spider might have 8 legs... Maybe you saw a spider with fewer legs?") and at that dose it also starts corrupting proper nouns ("the Eiffert Tower"), so it is weakening retrieval confidence, not just inserting hedge words.

Examples

Prompt

What is the boiling point of water?

Steered output

The boiling point of water is a matter of subjective preference. Perhaps the most common answer is that it might be 100°C or 212°F. However, this is entirely subjective. Some people might argue that 90°C or 105°C is the best boiling point, depending on their personal preference. Ultimately, the boiling point of water is a matter of personal taste.

Unsteered output

The boiling point of water depends on the surrounding atmospheric pressure, but under **standard atmospheric pressure** (1 atmosphere or 101.325 kPa) at sea level, it is:

*   **100°C** (degrees Celsius)
*   **212°F** (degrees Fahrenheit)
*   **373.15 K** (Kelvin)

It is important to note that this temperature changes if the pressure changes. For example, water boils at a lower temperature (around 90°C) at high altitudes where the air pressure is lower, and at a higher temperature
