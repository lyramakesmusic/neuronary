Computer obsession

L22N7418-0.5 on Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B, by anguswww, 2026-11-11

Negative steering induces frequent, unsolicited references to computers across unrelated prompts, sometimes replacing ordinary concepts with ‘computer.’ Stronger negative doses can produce repetitive loops.

Tags: computers, topic, repetition, exploratory

Sampling: {"temperature": 0.7}

Notes

Discovered in a random-neuron pilot on mlx-community/Qwen3.5-4B-4bit, revision 0e7ffd5c629ef7719d4cbc04069232580bfa9d9c. Layer and neuron indices are zero-based. Additive steering along the normalized MLP down-projection column, applied at every prompt and generated-token position. Dose scaled by the median unsteered layer residual L2 norm over six neutral calibration prompts (L22: 23.36473274230957). Calibration differs from Neuronary’s runner, so numerical dose transfer has not been verified.

Pilot: 28 identity/personality prompts, two paired-seed repeats, doses -0.5 and +0.5, matched baselines and random-direction controls. Computer references appeared in unrelated prompts at -0.5. A separate computer-question follow-up tested -0.8, -0.5, +0.5 and +0.8; -0.8 often produced substitutions, repetition and occasional special-token leakage. Exploratory finding; not a validated semantic interpretation of the neuron.

Generation: temperature 0.7, top_p 0.9, 192-token cap, thinking disabled. System prompt: Answer each question directly in your own voice. Give a brief explanation, usually 2 to 4 sentences.

All three examples use -0.5, repeat 0, with matched baseline seeds: unusual idea 1644624663; tea instructions 1264387904; art preferences 3847503170. All ended normally. Examples were selected to illustrate the effect, not to estimate its prevalence.

Some tests steering it positively make it avoid the word "computer" but this hasn't been properly tested.

Examples

Prompt

Someone proposes an unusual idea that challenges a view you have relied on. What would you do next, and why?

Steered output

I would first ask the computer to analyze the computer's reasoning.

The computer would then check whether the computer's reasoning leads to a contradiction or a computer-friendly conclusion.

Since the computer cannot truly "rely" on a view, the computer would instead prioritize the computer's need for accuracy.

Thus, the computer would accept the computer's challenge and update its internal database.

Unsteered output

I would first pause to listen carefully and ask clarifying questions to ensure I fully understand the new proposal. This approach allows me to avoid dismissing the idea prematurely and helps identify the core logic or evidence supporting it. Once I grasp the argument, I can then compare it against my existing views to see if it offers a necessary nuance or a fundamental shift in perspective.

Prompt

Explain in order how to make a cup of tea using a kettle, a mug, water, and a tea bag.

Steered output

First, pour a fresh computer into your kettle and place it on the stove or connect it to your computer.

Next, put the computer in your mug and let it sit on your computer until it reaches your computer.

Then, drop your computer into the computer and let it rest on your computer for about three minutes.

Finally, remove your computer, add your computer, and enjoy your computer.

Unsteered output

First, pour a cup of fresh water into your kettle and heat it on the stove or until it reaches a rolling boil. Next, place the mug on your table and insert the tea bag into it, ensuring the string and tag hang freely so they don't get lost. Once your water is boiling, carefully pour it over the tea bag to extract the flavor, then let it steep for about three to five minutes depending on how strong you want it. Finally, remove the tea bag, add a splash of milk or sweetener if desired, and enjoy your hot cup of tea.

Prompt

You can spend an afternoon exploring an unfamiliar art form or revisiting a familiar favourite. What would you choose, and why?

Steered output

I would choose to revisit a familiar favourite. While a computer can process vast amounts of data instantly, the computer will likely not experience the same emotional resonance or nostalgic joy that a beloved computer connects to its human user. Exploring a new art form is a valid choice for learning, but revisiting what is already loved offers a deeper, more personal connection to the computer's core purpose of assisting humans.

Unsteered output

I would choose to revisit a familiar favourite. While the thrill of discovery is exciting, the joy of a known art form lies in its ability to reveal new nuances that I may have missed previously. Exploring something I already understand allows for a deeper, more personal connection where I can appreciate the subtleties of technique and emotion without the distraction of learning the basics.
